# Licensing

This repository holds two quite different kinds of thing, and one licence cannot
sensibly cover both. Read this before assuming the badge on the repository page
tells you what you need.

| what | governed by |
|---|---|
| Tooling, CI, scripts, the website in `site/` | **AGPL-3.0** — see [`LICENSE`](LICENSE) |
| The choreography in `collections/` | Not copyrightable; see below |
| Written commentary in `collections/` | Its **author's** terms, declared per collection |

`LICENSE` contains the AGPL-3.0 text because that is what GitHub's licence
detection reads, and because it matches
[Caller's Compendium](https://github.com/ibanner56/CallersCompendium). **It
applies to the software in this repository, not to the collections.** Nothing
here places a published collection under the AGPL.

## Software — AGPL-3.0

Everything that is code: the publishing pipeline, the content gate, CI
workflows, and the static site under `site/`.

The app repository's `LICENSE-EXCEPTION.md` is deliberately **not** reproduced
here. That additional permission exists to allow distribution through app stores
whose terms conflict with the AGPL, which is a problem this repository does not
have — nothing here is distributed through a managed marketplace.

## Choreography — no copyright to license

The U.S. Copyright Office's *Compendium of U.S. Copyright Office Practices*
holds that the choreography of a social dance is not a copyrightable work.
Figures, sequences, formations, progressions and the other structural facts of a
dance therefore carry no copyright that anyone — including this project — could
license, restrict, or grant.

Stating that plainly matters more than it may seem. It is the reason a published
book's dances can be transcribed here at all, and the reason this repository
does not ask contributors to assign rights they do not have and could not
transfer.

**No additional rights are asserted over the assembled data.** Selecting and
arranging facts can attract a thin compilation right in some jurisdictions; this
project does not rely on any such right, and does not use one to restrict reuse
of the choreographic data published here.

## Written commentary — the author's, always

Teaching notes, histories, dedications, calling advice and similar prose are
**ordinary copyrighted works** and remain the property of whoever wrote them,
regardless of the status of the choreography they accompany.

A collection may include such material **only** where it carries a permission or
licence declaration naming who granted it and on what terms. That declaration —
not this file, and not `LICENSE` — governs the material. Where a collection
carries commentary under a public licence (CC BY, CC BY-NC and so on), the
declaration names it, and that licence is what applies.

Absence of a declaration is a hard failure in review, not a warning. See
[CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md).

## Attribution survives import

The permission and licence recorded for a collection travel with the dances into
the app, on each imported dance's provenance. Someone who imports a collection
can see who wrote a dance and under what terms its notes were shared, rather
than that context being lost at the boundary.

## Contributing

Contributions of **software** are accepted under the AGPL-3.0, matching this
repository's existing licence. You keep your copyright; you are licensing the
contribution on the same terms.

Contributing a **collection** is not a copyright grant, because for the
choreography there is nothing to grant. What you are asserting is narrower and
more important: that the attribution is right, and that any commentary present
is either yours or carries recorded permission from its author.

## If something here is wrong

If you believe material has been published without the permission it should
have, please email **compendium@contra.dance**. It will be handled promptly and
without a public thread. Because collections are immutable, a correction is
published as a new version — but a version can be superseded quickly, and
material published in error will be.
